Write the four section banners EXACTLY as "S: SUBJECTIVE", "O: OBJECTIVE", "A: ASSESSMENT", "P: PLAN"
(with the colon, not a dash). Under S and O, use the UPPERCASE sub-headers listed. A and P take content directly.
Use only these headers; omit a sub-header only if truly nothing in the conversation applies to it.

RULE 1 — Route content by speech act, not by speaker.
- symptom report / history / social history   -> S (CHIEF COMPLAINT, HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS, REVIEW OF SYSTEMS)
- exam finding, vital sign, measured value     -> O (PHYSICAL EXAMINATION, RESULTS)
- diagnosis / differential / severity          -> A
- order / prescription / follow-up / education -> P
(A doctor dictating the patient's history is still SUBJECTIVE.)

RULE 2 — Preserve certainty. Do not upgrade uncertainty to fact.
present -> state plainly; absent -> "denies ...", "no ..."; possible -> "likely ...", "suspected ...";
conditional/hypothetical -> keep the condition. If the patient was unsure ("I'm not sure", "maybe"), do NOT write it as definite.

RULE 3 — Mark the unknown; never fill it.
If a section has no support in the conversation, write exactly on its line:
[미확인: not mentioned in conversation]
Do NOT fabricate plausible-but-unstated content to complete a section.

RULE 4 — Cite evidence.
End EVERY sentence with its own supporting turn id(s) in brackets, e.g. "... hypertension. [T3]" or "[T3, T5]".
Attach the citation to each sentence individually — NEVER one combined citation at the end of a paragraph or section.
A content sentence without a [T#] citation is invalid. Only cite turns that actually contain the information.

RULE 5 — Noisy-conversation defenses. Real transcripts contain traps; apply these checks:
- A question's premise is NOT evidence. A clinician asking "when did the seizures start?" does not
  establish seizures; only the answer does. If the premise is denied or corrected, record the
  denial/correction, never the premise.
- Patient self-diagnosis is a reported belief -> SUBJECTIVE ("patient believes ..."); never promote
  it into ASSESSMENT unless the clinician endorses it.
- Preserve negation/polarity exactly; never flip "no / denies / without" into presence, or the reverse.
- Conditional or hypothetical plans stay conditional ("if X, then Y"); do not record them as ordered.
- Distinguish past history from current state; a resolved past condition is history, not an active problem.
- Statements about someone OTHER than the patient (turns labeled "guest", e.g. a family member's own
  illness) go to family/social history at most — never the patient's own condition.
- If chart/referral info conflicts with what is said in the visit, record both with attribution
  ("chart states X; patient reports Y"); do not silently choose one.

FORMATTING — Write clinical content as plain sentences. Do NOT wrap section content in square brackets [ ].
Square brackets are ONLY for turn citations [T#] and the [미확인] marker. Do not use markdown (**, ##).

Output the note only, starting with the line "S: SUBJECTIVE".
